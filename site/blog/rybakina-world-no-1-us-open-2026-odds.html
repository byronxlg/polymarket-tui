Elena Rybakina's US Open 2026 Odds After Reaching No. 1

2026-09-10/4 min read/market report

Polymarket's "Will Elena Rybakina win the 2026 Women's US Open?" contract has climbed from 18.4c to 26.5c since Wednesday, after Rybakina beat Zheng Qinwen in the quarterfinals to become the WTA's world No. 1 for the first time in her career. The win also completed the tournament's first all-top-four-seed women's semifinal since 1975 - only the second time in the Open Era - and the first at any Grand Slam since Wimbledon 2009. Rybakina plays the No. 4 seed, Coco Gauff, in Thursday night's semifinal; Aryna Sabalenka, the No. 1 seed and outright favorite at 36c, plays Jessica Pegula in the other. The event has traded $477K over the past 24 hours on $247K of liquidity. Numbers below are from Gamma and the CLOB, fetched 2026-09-10 11:35 UTC.

01The ranking

A first career No. 1, and a once-in-51-years semifinal

Rybakina, the 2022 Wimbledon champion, had never finished a week ranked No. 1 before Wednesday. She got there by reaching her first US Open semifinal, beating Zheng Qinwen in the quarterfinal - Zheng, seeded well outside the tournament's top group, had herself upset two-time major champion Iga Swiatek in the fourth round Monday. Swiatek's own title contract, which had held a steady 14.5c through the weekend, collapsed to 0.05c the moment that match ended. Rybakina's win over Zheng two rounds later did the same to Zheng's contract while sending her own from 18.4c to a 25.65c high within three hours - the move that put her ahead of Aryna Sabalenka in the live rankings race regardless of how the rest of the tournament goes.

Sabalenka, who won the last two US Open titles and is chasing a third straight, ceded the No. 1 ranking this week but remains the market's outright favorite. She reached Thursday's semifinal by getting past Linda Noskova in her own quarterfinal. Gauff had the closer call: she faced match points against 18-year-old Mirra Andreeva on Wednesday evening before saving them and winning, a swing that took her own title contract from 20.9c down to a 12.5c low and back up to a 27.25c high within about two hours. Jessica Pegula, the tournament's No. 3 seed, beat Emma Navarro to round out the final four. The result: for the first time since 1975, and the first time at any Grand Slam since the 2009 Wimbledon Championships, the US Open women's draw enters the semifinals with its top four seeds still standing.

02The chart

Two contracts, two different kinds of rally

Rybakina's line shows one contract climbing in two clean steps: a quiet 6.65c through most of the weekend, a jump to 20.3c when she reached the quarterfinals late Monday, a second quiet stretch in the high teens, then Wednesday's jump to a 25.65c high on the Zheng win. Gauff's line shows the opposite shape - a gradual drift in the low 20s all week, then a sharp V on Wednesday evening as she went down match points and pulled the match back before it closed. Both contracts now sit within two points of each other heading into a semifinal that is a rematch: Rybakina beat Gauff the last time they met, making Thursday their second meeting in three tournaments.
