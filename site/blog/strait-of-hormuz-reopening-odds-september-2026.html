Strait of Hormuz Reopening Odds on Polymarket: September 2026

2026-08-26/5 min read/market report

Monday, August 24, 2026, the Trump administration launched "Operation Economic Outcast," a sweeping new round of sanctions meant to cut Iran off from the global financial system. A day later Polymarket's Iran de-escalation odds went up, not down. As of 2026-08-26 07:16 UTC, "US announces end of Iranian blockade by September 30?" trades 40c bid / 41c ask, up from roughly 29.5c a day earlier, after Oman's foreign minister flew to Tehran and both governments said a temporary Strait of Hormuz shipping corridor could be announced soon. Numbers below are from Gamma and the CLOB, fetched at that time.

01Two headlines, one week

A sanctions campaign that didn't move the odds it should have

Operation Economic Outcast is a real escalation: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent added five more sectors of Iran's economy - aviation, digital assets, gold, shipping, and technology - to five already under sweeping sectoral sanctions, blacklisted more than 60 Iran-linked targets, and explicitly threatened secondary sanctions on any company or government anywhere that keeps trading with Iran, calling it "an economic onslaught against Iran's financial connections around the globe." That is the loud headline of the week, and Polymarket's blockade-end contract barely reacted to it - it spent Monday drifting in a tight 29-33c band and closed the day lower than it opened. The quieter story moved the price: Tuesday, Oman's foreign minister Badr Albusaidi met Iranian counterpart Abbas Araghchi in Tehran to discuss a "phased framework" for a temporary Hormuz shipping corridor and a mine-clearing project. No deal was signed, but Albusaidi called the talks productive and said an announcement could come "soon" - and that is exactly when the ladder turned.

02The chart

The move traces to Tehran, not Washington

The blockade-end contract sat within a 4-point band through all of Monday's sanctions rollout, bottomed at 28.5c overnight, then climbed steadily from 08:30 UTC Tuesday - roughly when wire reports on Albusaidi's Tehran meeting started circulating - to a 43c intraday peak by 10:00, before easing back to settle near 40.5c. The traffic-normal contract, thinner and further from even money, tells the same story at a different scale: flat at 5.5c through Monday, then a staircase of steps through Tuesday and into Wednesday morning to 11.5c - roughly double, on the same corridor talks.

Every date on the ladder is green, and the biggest point gain sits at October 31 - a date far enough out that a temporary shipping corridor now, followed by a fuller deal later this autumn, is a plausible enough path to reprice by 13 points in a day.

04Same story, three ladders

Blockade, traffic, and ceasefire aren't the same bet - but they moved together

Polymarket runs three separate series on this crisis, each resolving on a different trigger. The blockade-end ladder above resolves on a US government announcement. The traffic-normal contract resolves on IMF Portwatch data showing ships actually moving through the strait again, regardless of what either government says - it's at 11.5c (+6.0c, roughly double) for September 30. A third series, "US ceasefire against Iran continues through...?", just asks whether the fighting stays paused: September 15 is at 89.5c (+8.0c), September 30 at 83.5c (+10.0c), October 31 at 73.5c (+7.0c). None of these three would have to move together - a blockade could end while traffic stays disrupted for weeks, or the ceasefire could hold while Washington and Tehran stay at a diplomatic standstill. That they all repriced up on the same two days is the market treating the Oman channel as evidence about the whole conflict, not just the shipping question it was actually about.

05Reading the book

Real size, and one place it's pushing back

The blockade-end book is thin on both sides relative to the $347,889 that changed hands on this contract in the past 24 hours - a fast repricing that mostly consumed resting liquidity rather than one that ground through a deep book. The traffic-normal book tells a sharper story: 25,561 shares resting on the 12c ask against just 3,472 on the 11c bid, an eight-to-one imbalance. Someone has real size positioned for this rally to stall right at 12c - worth watching alongside the headlines, since a wall like that in an otherwise thin, no-deal-yet market is itself a signal.

06Watching three ladders at once

From the terminal, not three browser tabs

A single crisis spread across three related events, each with its own dated ladder, is easy to lose track of one tab at a time. In polymarket-tui, the watchlist keeps all three events side by side, and each event's outcome table lists every date's price and 24-hour move together - so a diplomatic meeting in Tehran that moves a blockade contract, a shipping-traffic contract, and a ceasefire contract all in the same afternoon shows up as three rows going green at once, not three separate refreshes.
