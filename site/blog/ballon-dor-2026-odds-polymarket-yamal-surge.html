Ballon d'Or 2026 Polymarket Odds: Kane Fades, Yamal Surges

2026-09-28/5 min read/market report

Voting for the 2026 Ballon d'Or closed today, and Polymarket's market on the winner has moved hard over the last 48 hours: Harry Kane, the favorite since France Football's 30-man shortlist came out on September 8, has faded from 58c to 50c, while Lamine Yamal has surged from a flat 22c to 36c. The public storyline is a Yamal-vs-Mbappé fight - Yamal has spent two weeks framing it that way himself - but the market disagrees about who Yamal is actually taking share from. Numbers below are from Gamma and the CLOB, fetched 2026-09-28 14:14 UTC.

01The campaign

"Me and Mbappé" - except the market never priced it that way

On September 14, Barcelona's Lamine Yamal told Argentine legend Jorge Valdano on Movistar+: "I think maybe there are two of us, me and Mbappé, but I think this year I deserve it because of what I've won" - leaning on Spain's 2026 World Cup title as his case over Rodri, Haaland, and the rest of the 30-man shortlist. Real Madrid's Kylian Mbappé, who won the World Cup Golden Boot as the tournament's all-time top scorer despite France falling short of the trophy, responded Sunday, September 27, calling the 19-year-old Yamal "an extremely talented player" and wishing him a brilliant career - the closest thing to an acknowledgment of a rivalry from either side.

Polymarket's own ladder never bought the two-man framing. Harry Kane - 61 goals in 51 games for Bayern Munich, even though England's World Cup run ended before the final - has been the outright favorite since the shortlist dropped, and Mbappé has traded in the single digits for weeks, well behind not just Kane and Yamal but also Lionel Messi. The move that actually happened this week is Kane losing ground and Yamal picking it up - not Yamal closing in on Mbappé.

02The chart

Yamal's rise started before Mbappé said a word

Both contracts sat almost flat for the first ten days after Yamal's interview - Kane chopping in the mid-to-high 50s, Yamal stuck around 20-23c - while the "me or Mbappé" line got repeated across the football press without moving a cent. The real break came late on September 26, more than a day before Mbappé's response even landed: Yamal jumped from 22.1c to 26.9c in a matter of hours, then kept climbing through the weekend and into this morning as voting entered its final stretch, reaching 33.75c by noon UTC today and 36.3c by the time of writing. Kane's decline runs the other direction across the same window - roughly flat through most of last week, then losing 8 points since yesterday morning alone, including a 3-point drop in the hour before this post. The two lines are trading against each other almost point for point on the way down and up.

The order books show the same split. Kane's contract is a tight 1c-wide market with real size on both sides - 912 shares bid at 50c against 999 offered at 50.1c - the kind of book you'd expect around a favorite with weeks of steady trading behind it. Yamal's book is wider and thinner right at the touch (155 shares bid at 35.7c, 355 offered at 36.9c, though size picks up a cent back on either side), consistent with a contract that's been repricing fast rather than settling into a level. Mbappé's book, by contrast, is deep and boring - thousands of shares resting a tenth of a cent apart around 5.4c, a market that isn't expecting news. The event has traded $784,592 over the past 24 hours and $2.33M over the past week against $8.5M of total liquidity across all 30 candidates, with 1,062 comments on the thread.

04What would move it next

The vote is already in; the reveal is October 26

Ballon d'Or voting works like a ranked ballot: 100 journalists, one per eligible country, each rank ten of the 30 shortlisted players, with a 15-12-10-8-7-6-5-4-2-1 points scale rewarding first place far more than tenth. That vote closed today, September 28 - ten days after France Football's own quoted deadline, per Ballon d'Or organizers - which means every trade on this market from here to the ceremony is pure speculation about ballots that have already been cast and locked, not a reaction to anything a player can still do on the pitch. The winner won't be revealed until the ceremony on October 26 at the London Palladium - the first time the event has been staged there - so the next four weeks are the market trying to guess a result that's already fixed rather than pricing a race still in motion.
