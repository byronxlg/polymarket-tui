Bitcoin $87,500 Odds on Polymarket: September 2026 Whipsaw

2026-09-27/5 min read/market report

Polymarket's "Will Bitcoin reach $85,000 in September?" contract has now resolved Yes three separate times this month, as bitcoin kept crossing the level and falling back below it before a fresh contract re-listed to cover the rest of the month. With the ladder closing out at the end of trading September 30, the live rungs above it are pricing a real shot at $87,500 (30c) and even $90,000 (9c), while a separate contract on a dip back to $82,500 sits at 35c - genuine two-sided risk with days left on the clock. Numbers below are from Gamma and the CLOB, and bitcoin's own price from CoinGecko and Coinbase, all fetched 2026-09-27 12:20 UTC.

01How it got here

From a 21-month low to an $18B options expiry

Bitcoin bottomed at $57,950 on July 1, 2026 - its lowest level in 21 months - then found a second, higher low near $62,210 on August 1 before running to $81,265 by the early hours of August 25, a move this blog covered as it happened. The climb kept going into September: bitcoin cleared $87,000 on September 22 as roughly $648 million in short positions were liquidated, a squeeze that followed close to $1 billion in ETF creations the day before and left bitcoin up 13-14% on the week, per Bloomberg and Benzinga reporting. That run fed directly into Friday's (September 25) options expiry - $15.9 billion in Bitcoin contracts settling at 8:00 UTC, the largest single expiry of 2026, wiping out roughly 37% of Deribit's outstanding BTC open interest in one shot. Deribit's own positioning data showed the heaviest concentrations of call open interest sitting at exactly $85,000, $90,000, and $100,000 - the same round-number levels Polymarket's ladder is built on.

As of 2026-09-27 12:20 UTC, bitcoin trades at roughly $84,980 (CoinGecko $84,989, Coinbase $84,982) - up about 47% from July's $57,950 low, but still well under the roughly $126,000 cycle high it touched in October 2025.

02The chart

Three trips across $85,000 in a week

The $87,500 contract spent the weekend of September 20-21 in the mid-20s, then jumped past 90c by September 23 as bitcoin's short squeeze carried it to the edge of the strike - only for the price to fall back before actually touching $87,500, so the contract never resolved. It settled into the 20s-30s for the rest of the week as bitcoin chopped below the level, then climbed back to 30c as of this writing. The $90,000 contract traced the same shape at a smaller scale, peaking near 52c before fading to 9c. Underneath both, the $85,000 rung actually did get touched - three times. Gamma's records show separate $85,000 contracts closing Yes on September 21 (11:12 UTC), September 25 (11:33 UTC), and again this morning, September 27 (10:36 UTC): each time bitcoin crossed $85,000, that contract resolved and a fresh one opened to cover the rest of the month, and each time bitcoin has slipped back below the level within a day or two - which is exactly what the still-open "dip to $85,000" and "dip to $82,500" contracts are pricing.

04What would move it next

Three days, and a ladder that keeps resetting

The September ladder closes at 2026-10-01 04:00 UTC - effectively the end of the trading day September 30 in the US. That leaves roughly three days for bitcoin to either clear $87,500 for the first time this month (30c says it's a real possibility, not a near-lock) or slip back through $82,500 (35c says that's just as live). The rolling-threshold mechanic that produced three separate $85,000 resolutions this week means it could easily happen a fourth time before Wednesday if bitcoin keeps chopping around current levels - each crossing is its own settlement, not a one-time coin flip for the month.
