LeBron to Philadelphia 76ers: What the Signing Moved

2026-07-25/5 min read/market report

LeBron James posted his decision on X Friday morning: a two-year, $8 million deal with the Philadelphia 76ers, the team he called his "last decision." Polymarket's "NBA: LeBron James Next Team" market didn't drift toward the news - it snapped. The 76ers' Yes price went from 8.3c to 98.8c in a five-minute window, and every other team's line collapsed in the same stretch. Two days earlier this market's loudest signal had been a Miami Heat social-media leak; James went to Philadelphia instead. Numbers below are from the Gamma and CLOB APIs, fetched 2026-07-25 08:30 UTC.

01The board right now

One team left standing

Polymarket splits "who signs LeBron" into a separate Yes/No market per team. A day ago that board had a favorite and a long tail; now it has one market at 99.55c and everything else near zero:

LEBRON JAMES NEXT TEAM                PRICE    24H
Philadelphia 76ers                    99.55c    +91.00c
Miami Heat                             0.15c    -42.95c
Cleveland Cavaliers                    0.05c    -25.20c
Golden State Warriors                  0.25c    -17.00c
Minnesota Timberwolves                 0.05c     -3.50c

The event has traded $4.40M in the last 24 hours - more than any other market on Polymarket right now except the Fed's July decision. Miami, Cleveland, and Golden State were the names reporters kept floating all offseason (Minnesota, Milwaukee, and a handful of others also had live but thinner markets); the Los Angeles Lakers, the default outcome if no signing happened by the market's October 31 deadline, are down to 0.05c too. There is functionally one outcome left being traded here.

02What actually happened

A last decision, made official fast

James left the Lakers this offseason without naming a destination, and had "no shortage of suitors," per reporting that included the Heat, Warriors, and Timberwolves among the teams making a pitch. Two days before the signing, Miami's own social media team briefly posted a since-deleted "LeBron James Introductory Press Conference" video - a leak the team called invalid, and which Polymarket's Heat market faded most of within a day (see our July 23 post on that spike). The real answer went the other way. James's contract with Philadelphia runs $3,876,529 for 2026-27 and $4,070,355 for 2027-28, both under the veteran minimum exception. Pennsylvania governor Josh Shapiro proclaimed July 24 "LeBron James Day" within hours of the announcement - the kind of fast, public confirmation that leaves little room for the signing to unwind.

03The five-minute reprice

Flat for two days, then vertical

The line sat at 7.3-8.3c for two full days, drifting inside a one-cent band the way a thin, speculative contract does. At 15:35 UTC on July 24 - late morning on the US East Coast, matching when James's post went up - it jumped to 98.8c inside five minutes and has barely moved since, closing today at 99.55c. Compare that shape to the Heat leak two days earlier: that spike built over 30 minutes and gave back half its gain over the next day as doubt set in. This move didn't build and it hasn't given anything back - the difference between a rumor repricing a probability and a confirmed fact repricing a certainty.

04The book behind 99.55c

A tight new market sitting on top of an abandoned one

The 76ers' order book today quotes a one-tenth-cent-wide 99.5c/99.6c, with $349 resting on the bid and $412 on the ask - a normal, tight book for a contract everyone agrees is settled. But it's sitting on top of 61 bid levels left over from before the jump, stretching down to 0.1c: resting orders from traders who priced the 76ers as a long shot and never got a chance to cancel before the news made them irrelevant. Miami's book shows the mirror image - a single bid at 0.1c and 80 ask levels stacked above it, the shape of a market nobody wants to trade anymore. Between them, the two books are a fast way to see which side of a repriced market still has live interest and which is just debris.

05What's left before it's official

0.45c of "not yet," not "maybe not"

The market is still open - Polymarket's own listing shows it active, not closed - because the contract resolves on an "official acquisition announcement," and Gamma hasn't yet flagged one despite the 76ers' own announcement, NBA.com's coverage, and a governor's proclamation all saying the same thing. That's the same lag this blog covered after the World Cup final: real-world resolution and market resolution are two different clocks, and the second one runs behind the first by design, not by doubt. The 0.45c still priced on "No" isn't the market hedging against James changing his mind; it's the cost of a contract that hasn't formally settled yet, on a market that would rather be a cent slow than wrong.

06Watching a market like this move

Every team's line, one screen
