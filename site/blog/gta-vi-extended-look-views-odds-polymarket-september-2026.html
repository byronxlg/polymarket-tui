GTA VI Extended Look Views Odds on Polymarket: September 2026

2026-09-03/4 min read/market report

Rockstar posted "Grand Theft Auto VI: An Extended Look" to its YouTube channel on August 27, 2026 at 6:00pm PT (01:00 UTC August 28). Polymarket runs a market on exactly one number from that video: how many views it racks up on Rockstar's channel in its first 168 hours. As of 2026-09-03 11:27 UTC the counter reads 20,341,522 - and the market's bracket for "20 to 22 million" now prices at 90.5c, a contract that was worth 35.5c as recently as Tuesday afternoon. Numbers below are from Gamma, the CLOB, and Rockstar's own YouTube page, fetched at that time.

01The market

Twelve brackets, one YouTube counter

The event, "# of views of Grand Theft Auto VI Extended Look on week 1?", is a ladder of twelve mutually exclusive 2-million-wide brackets running from "under 20 million" up to "40 million or more." Each bracket is its own Yes/No market, so the ladder behaves like a histogram: money buying one bracket is a bet against all eleven others. The resolution source is explicit and narrow - "the 'views' counter for the described video" on the Rockstar Games YouTube channel, not Netflix's separate release of the same footage, which draws its own tally on a different platform and isn't counted here. The market opened the day after the video went up and is listed to close 2026-09-03 23:59 UTC, close to the 168-hour mark from the video's actual publish timestamp.

02The chart

A quiet week, then a fast flip

For the video's first three days the ladder just drifted - both brackets traded in a wide, choppy 15-45c range with no clear favorite, reflecting genuine uncertainty over where a Rockstar trailer viewed live on Netflix as well as YouTube would land. That changed around 16:20 UTC Tuesday, September 2: "under 20 million" dropped from 53.5c to 26.5c in a single ten-minute print while "20 to 22 million" jumped from 44.5c to 72c. The two lines swap places on the chart within the same window and never look back - by Wednesday morning "under 20 million" is a fraction of a cent and "20 to 22 million" holds a steady 85-90c band.

03What flipped it

The counter did the work, not a news event

There's no headline behind Tuesday afternoon's move - no Rockstar statement, no viral moment. It's arithmetic. A video accumulating views on a fixed clock has a running pace, and once enough of the 168-hour window had elapsed with the counter climbing steadily, "under 20 million" stopped being a plausible outcome well before the deadline arrived. That's a different shape of story than most of what this blog covers - no single event to point to, just a market pricing in a trend line days ahead of the number it's tracking actually closing in on the bracket boundary. The video's own view count, read directly from Rockstar's channel page at 2026-09-03 11:27 UTC, sits at 20,341,522 - inside the bracket the market now prices at 90.5c, and up from a pace that had "under 20 million" as the narrow favorite less than 24 hours earlier.

04The rest of the ladder

A thin tail still prices some upside

The neighboring brackets show the shape of the remaining uncertainty more clearly than the leader does on its own:

"22 to 24 million" is the market's residual tail risk: with roughly 12 hours left on the clock as of this writing, there's still a real chance the counter's pace carries it past 22 million before the window closes, and that contract's own 24-hour move (+8.5 points) shows traders adding to that possibility as "under 20 million" died off, not just abandoning the dead bracket. The order book for "20 to 22 million" backs up the picture: a 90c bid for just 14 shares against a 91c ask stacked with 760 shares - a one-cent spread, but thin on the buy side, exactly what you'd expect 14 hours from a still-moving target rather than a settled coin flip.

05Watching a bracket ladder

Twelve markets, one screen

A bracket ladder like this one is really twelve separate order books that only make sense read together - the interesting number is never one contract's price in isolation, it's how the whole distribution shifted. In polymarket-tui, opening the event lists every bracket with its live price and 24-hour move in one table, so a flip like Tuesday's shows up as a wave moving down the ladder instead of requiring twelve separate lookups.
